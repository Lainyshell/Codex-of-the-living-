# Why We Built This

**Author:** Alaina Padgett, System Steward
**For:** My family, the Verdigris Botanica Tribal Nation, and everyone who will steward this after me
**Date:** 2026-08-06

---

## The Short Version

I built this because the Nation needed infrastructure that belonged to us — not to a vendor, not to a platform, not to any individual. Something that works for our people and cannot be turned against them.

---

## The Longer Version

### Where We Started

The Verdigris Botanica Tribal Nation has always operated in a world that wasn't designed for us. The systems built for financial management, document execution, governance records, and legal infrastructure were built by and for institutions that don't share our values, our sovereignty, or our history.

When you use someone else's tools, you live by their rules. Your data lives in their house. Your records are subject to their terms. Your people's information flows through systems that may not understand — or may actively resist — tribal sovereignty.

That's not good enough. Not for this Nation.

---

### What I Set Out to Build

I wanted to build something that was **ours**. Not rented. Not borrowed. Not dependent on someone else's goodwill.

Specifically, I wanted:
- **Financial infrastructure** — tools for managing the Nation's money, disbursements, and records that belonged to us
- **Document infrastructure** — the ability to execute and track legal instruments under our own governance, not someone else's
- **Governance infrastructure** — records and systems that supported our sovereignty rather than quietly eroding it

And I wanted these things to **outlast me**.

---

### The Hardest Design Problem

Technical problems are solvable. You write code, you test it, you fix it.

The hardest problem was this: **What happens to the Nation's infrastructure if I'm gone?**

If it's tied to my account, it disappears when I do.
If it's tied to a vendor's terms, it disappears when they change those terms.
If it's not documented, it becomes a black box that nobody else can operate.
If it's not governed, it can be captured, sold, or turned against the very people it was meant to serve.

I spent a lot of time thinking about those risks. This system — the governance documents, the legal instruments, the technical safeguards, the training materials — is my answer to them.

---

### The Lines That Must Never Be Crossed

This system was built to serve our members. It must never be used to:

**Surveil them.** I built it knowing that surveillance technology is often turned first and hardest against communities like ours. This system will never become a tool for watching people.

**Score them.** Behavioral scoring — even when it's called something neutral, like "risk assessment" — is a way of sorting people into categories that determine how they're treated. We don't do that here.

**Sell their data.** Our people's information is not a commodity. It was shared with the Nation in trust. It leaves only under the explicit authority of a Tribal Resolution — never as a product, never quietly, never to enrich a vendor.

**Target them.** No entity — including any government agency — may use this system as a weapon against our members. If someone tries, the system will resist. And if it can't resist technically, the governance structure will resist legally.

These aren't just rules. They are the reason this system exists. A system that crosses these lines is no longer the Codex of the Living — it's something else entirely, and it should be shut down.

---

### What I Hope This Becomes

I hope this becomes infrastructure that serves the Nation for generations.

I hope that someday the Tribal Technical Lead is someone who grew up in this community and learned to run this system from the training materials I wrote.

I hope the Stewardship Circle becomes a genuine community of people who understand what this system does, what it's for, and what it will never do — and who feel the authority and the responsibility to protect it.

I hope that when hard decisions come — and they will — the governance structure I've built makes it easier for the people who come after me to make the right choices.

I hope this system serves the Nation faithfully and is never turned against it.

---

### A Note on Imperfection

This system is not perfect. The code has bugs I haven't found. The governance documents have gaps I haven't anticipated. The legal instruments need review by qualified counsel I haven't fully engaged.

What I can say is that every decision I made, I made with the Nation's interests in mind. Where I got it wrong, I hope the people who come after me have the tools and the authority to fix it.

Don't let reverence for what I built stop you from improving it. Just make sure that when you change it, you change it for the Nation's benefit — not for convenience, not for someone else's benefit, and never in ways that cross the lines I've described above.

---

### To the People Who Will Steward This After Me

You are the reason I wrote all of this down.

I wanted to make sure that if something happened to me, the Nation wouldn't lose this. I wanted the people I trust to be able to pick this up and keep it running — and to know why it was built the way it was built.

You have authority over this system. Use it carefully, use it collectively, and use it always in the service of the Nation.

The system belongs to the people. Never forget that.

— Alaina
